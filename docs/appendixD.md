# Appendix D. Real-firm robustness

A natural objection is that the instability reflects our constructed inputs. But evaluating LLMs on real issuers is itself contaminated: asked to name the company behind an anonymized financial profile, Gemini-Flash identifies 22% of mid-cap issuers from the figures alone ("≈$14.7B revenue, large loss, negative equity…"), clustered and reasoning-traced. Naive real-firm evaluation therefore measures recall, not judgment — independent grounds for the constructed battery.

We nonetheless build a decontaminated arm: 31 U.S. mid-cap ($2–15B) business-to-business issuers whose latest 10-K discloses a current agency rating (verbatim quote, SEC URL, document hash). Each dollar figure is scaled by a per-issuer factor drawn log-uniform[0.6, 1.7] — preserving every ratio, hence the rating task, while breaking figure lookup — and consumer-facing firms are screened out. A pre-registered fingerprinting gate certifies decontamination at 2.9% identification. (One scale drew ≈1.0, leaving an issuer near-raw; the gate returned zero identifications for it, so its decontamination is shown empirically rather than by perturbation.)

Rating each firm across a frozen 12-specification grid at three seeds, the WATCH-stratum band flip-share is 0.23 (95% CI 0.15–0.31) versus 0.32 (0.22–0.41) for the battery on the identical grid — a difference of −0.09 (95% CI −0.21, 0.03), inside the pre-registered ±0.15 equivalence bound. Instability replicates on real issuers. The lower point estimate is consistent with real WATCH names sitting farther from band thresholds, not with residual memorization. Specification moves the band even at temperature zero, while model bands match the disclosed agency rating only 54% of the time (descriptive).
